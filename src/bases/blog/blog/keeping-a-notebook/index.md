I have filled forty-one notebooks since 2015. Most of what is in them is useless: shopping lists, half a phone number, a drawing of a chair I never built.

That is the point. A notebook that only holds good ideas stays empty.

![An open notebook on a desk](./images/lead.svg)

## The one rule

Nothing in the notebook has to be good. I wrote that inside the cover of the first one, and I have copied it into each one since.

> A page you are afraid to spoil is a page you will not use.

The rule removes the decision. I do not ask whether a thought deserves the page. I write it down and move on.

## What goes in

- Things people say that I want to remember exactly
- Measurements, because I always forget them
- The first bad version of anything I plan to write

## What I learned from reading them back

Each January I read the notebooks from the year before. Three patterns appear each time:

1. The ideas I was most excited about are rarely the ones I finished.
2. The small complaints repeat until I fix their cause.
3. I am kinder on paper than I remember being.

If you want to start, buy a cheap notebook. An expensive one will make you careful, and careful is the opposite of what you want.
